Oakbook Security Overview

How your vault is protected, in detail

In short

  • Your vault is one file, encrypted with 256-bit authenticated encryption: AES-256-GCM by default, or XChaCha20-Poly1305 if you choose it.
  • The key is made from your master password with Argon2id, which makes every password guess slow and memory-hungry.
  • Your master password is never stored, and your vault never goes online.

How your vault is locked

  1. You choose a master password (at least 10 characters).
  2. Oakbook turns it into a 256-bit key with Argon2id, using a random 16-byte salt.
  3. Your logins, notes and two-factor keys are encrypted with that key and saved as a single file on your iPhone.
  4. When you lock Oakbook, the key is erased from memory. Without your master password (or your Face ID, if you turned it on), the file can't be opened.

Encryption: two choices, one default

Oakbook offers two authenticated ciphers. Both use a 256-bit key and give the same practical level of protection: neither can be broken by trying keys. "Authenticated" means any change to the file, even one byte, is detected, and the vault simply refuses to open instead of showing altered data.

Which should I pick? Keep the default unless you have a specific reason. Your master password matters far more than the choice of cipher.

Switching later: in Settings ▸ Encryption, enter your master password and pick the other cipher. Oakbook re-encrypts the whole vault, with a new salt and key, and deletes the old copy. A vault uses one cipher at a time. Encrypted backups keep the cipher the vault had when you exported them, and Oakbook reads the setting from each file, so any backup opens either way.

Every time the vault is saved, it gets a fresh random nonce. The file's settings (format version, cipher and key-strength values) are also authenticated, so they can't be tampered with either.

Your master password: Argon2id

Argon2id is a password-hashing method designed to make guessing expensive: each guess needs a lot of memory and time, which slows down attackers who copy your vault file and try billions of passwords on powerful hardware. You choose how strong when you create the vault, and you can change it in Settings ▸ Encryption.

Oakbook also refuses vault files whose key settings are outside safe limits, so a tampered file can't force a weak key.

Face ID, Touch ID and AutoFill

  • If you turn on Face ID or Touch ID, the vault key is kept in your iPhone's Keychain behind a Secure Enclave rule: iOS releases it only after a successful biometric match.
  • That key never leaves the device and is never synced to iCloud. iOS deletes it if you add a new face or fingerprint, or remove your passcode, and you then unlock with your master password.
  • Oakbook AutoFill (a Pro feature) reads the same encrypted file and gets the key only through Face ID. It decrypts with Apple's CryptoKit and never sees your master password.

Other protections on your iPhone

  • iOS Data Protection: the vault file is also protected by your iPhone's own file encryption while the phone is locked.
  • Auto-lock: after 2 minutes without use by default, and whenever you leave the app.
  • Wrong passwords: after 3 wrong attempts, Oakbook makes you wait, starting at 5 seconds and doubling up to 5 minutes.
  • Clipboard: copied passwords stay on this iPhone (no Universal Clipboard) and clear after 30 seconds by default.
  • Screen: contents are hidden in the app switcher and while the screen is recorded or mirrored.
  • Your vault never goes online: Oakbook contains no networking code, no analytics and no ads, and every vault feature works offline. The only internet connections are Apple's App Store services for Oakbook Pro: showing the price, buying, restoring purchases, redeeming codes, and Apple's own background purchase checks. They never include your vault or its contents.

What Oakbook can't protect against

  • A device that is already compromised: malware, a jailbroken phone, or someone watching you type.
  • Someone who knows your master password.
  • A forgotten master password: it can't be recovered or reset, by anyone.
  • CSV files you import are not encrypted. Delete them after importing.

How this is checked

The app uses the open-source cryptography library for Argon2id and both ciphers; AutoFill uses Apple CryptoKit. Every build runs automated tests: encryption round trips for both ciphers, wrong-password and tampered-file rejection, a fresh nonce on every save, and a check that the AutoFill code opens vaults written by the app. A separate scan confirms the app contains no networking or analytics code.
